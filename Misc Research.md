Effect of manufacture conditions on reverse osmosis desalination performance of polyamide thin film composite membrane and their spiral wound element

Main findings

    Manufacturing conditions matter at both stages of production. The authors varied the operating conditions of the casting and coating machines and found that these conditions affected the RO performance of the flat-sheet membrane
    1
    .
    Membrane characterization supported successful TFC formation. The fabricated flat-sheet membranes were analyzed by ATR-FTIR, contact angle, dynamic mechanical analysis, and SEM, confirming that structure and surface properties were being changed during manufacture
    1
    .
    Element fabrication improved performance. After the flat sheet was rolled into a spiral-wound element, the RO performance improved significantly, indicating that element manufacturing itself contributed to better desalination behavior
    1
    .
    The membrane was tested under realistic conditions. Performance was evaluated using synthesized NaCl solution as well as natural brackish and saline groundwater or seawater samples, so the study connected manufacturing effects to practical desalination use
