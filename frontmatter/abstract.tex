\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Reverse osmosis (RO) membranes are replaced every five to nine years, and almost all end-of-life (EoL) elements are sent to landfill or incineration, even though most of the damage is limited to the thin polyamide (PA) layer. This thesis evaluated whether EoL RO membranes can be regenerated by forming a new PA layer by interfacial polymerisation (IP) without stripping the old one, and how fouling and chemical cleaning before IP affect performance. Three EoL elements were tested at coupon scale: two from a seawater desalination plant (4238A and 4238C) and one from a mine site (4297B). Coupons were tested as received, after a commercial or extensive alkaline--acid clean, and after \textit{m}-phenylenediamine/trimesoyl chloride IP. Pure water permeability (PWP), and NaCl rejection were measured, and selected coupons were characterised by ATR-FTIR and SEM.\\

Cleaning alone did not significantly change PWP or rejection on any membrane. After the extensive clean and IP, PWP fell by \qty{89}{\percent} on 4238C and \qty{86}{\percent} on 4297B, but rejection did not change. FTIR confirmed new PA on 4238C, but $B$ fell by about the same fraction as PWP, so the new layer only added resistance without adding selectivity. The only condition in which $B$ fell by more than PWP was 4238C with no clean before IP, where rejection rose by \qty{10.3}{pp} to \qty{86.9}{\percent}. This result came from a preliminary IP run and is indicative only.\\

IP can form a new PA layer on unstripped EoL membranes, but an extensive clean beforehand did not help restore selectivity.  Future work should repeat IP without a clean with more replicates, use in-situ IP in modules so localised damage is averaged out, test a commercial clean on more membranes, and optimise IP conditions for aged PA surfaces.
