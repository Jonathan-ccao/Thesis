\section{Background and Motivation}
An accelerated global development of reverse osmosis (RO) membranes has created a rapidly growing volume of end-of-life (EoL) membrane waste that is difficult to manage through existing disposal pathways. As membrane-based desalination and water treatment gains increasing investment, the quantity of membranes requiring replacement has also grown proportionally. However, most membranes continue to be disposed of to landfill or incineration, and presents a missed opportunity for material recovery and reuse. 
 


\section{Problem statement}
- EoL membranes are landfillled/ incinerated; downcycling (RO to NF/UF) is not ideal; regeneration is under explored

\section{Research aims}
- Evaluate whether EoL RO membranes can be uplifted by despositing a new polyamid 
layer via IP, and how cleaning intensity beforehand can affect the result 

\section{Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Characterise EoL RO membranes and Fouling Profiles
  \item Clean RO membrane
  \item Interfacial polymerisation 
  \item PWP + NaCL rejection across 3 arms x 2 membranes
  \item Autopsy selected coupons
\end{enumerate}
